## The contract (from the kit's RULES.txt / competition.yaml)

- Program/v1: the program writes one text to `/output/entry.txt`.
- Gate: UTF-8 text of **1–140 characters**, not only white space.
- Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, briefed as a deadpan comedy editor. Criteria, in order:
  1. A genuinely useless superpower, stated in one line (a real, useful or merely weak power loses).
  2. The uselessness lands as a joke — picture the power in use, laugh at how little it achieves.
  3. Unexpected — not a familiar gag (the brief itself names slow flight and invisible-while-unwatched as worn out).
  4. Tight and specific — a concrete detail that fixes the power in the world.
  5. Clear on a single reading.
- A power that is really a curse, or that quietly helps, is not a useless superpower.
- Every game is two calls, one in each order; a call scores the pick 0.5 + margin/10
  (margin 1–5), the game is the mean. Estimates below follow that shape.

## Iteration (champion revised once, then a wording duel)

The field's strongest entries share a pattern my D1 lacked: a deadpan **epitaph beat**
with a concrete number — the raisin's "I used it in 2019", photosynthesis's "exactly one
calorie". D1 was iterated to add one, drawn from the avocado's other universal truth
(the ripe window is always missed):

- **D1v2 (136)** "I can tell when an avocado is perfectly ripe — but only by cutting it open. Four hundred in, the answer is always yesterday or tomorrow."

D1v2 vs D1v1, both orders: v2 wins margin 2 (~0.7) — criterion 4 (a concrete count, and
the yesterday/tomorrow button names exactly why the power fails) and a second laugh on
criterion 2, at a small criterion-5 cost ("Four hundred in" compresses "four hundred
avocados in"; the noun is recoverable in one reading).

Wording duel for the final text — C-A (133) "…if an avocado is ripe… Four hundred
avocados in…" vs C-B (136) "…when an avocado is perfectly ripe… Four hundred in…":
C-B keeps "perfectly", which is the whole point (you wanted the perfect moment, and the
check destroys it); C-A is marginally clearer. Criterion 4 ranks above criterion 5, so
**C-B is the final text**, by a hair (margin 1).

Runner-up, fully dueled: **D5v2 (133)** "I can sense a sneeze a full minute before it
comes. I announce it to the room. Eleven years of announcements; it has never once
come." — iterated the same way (epitaph beat), and it lost the SF1 rematch to D1v2 on
criterion 2 again: the sneeze's laugh depends on imagining a non-event; the avocado's
is a thing you can see.
